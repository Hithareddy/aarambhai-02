## 1. Learning Goals

### 1.1 Basic Reading & Writing

**Objective:** Enable learners with limited or no formal education to develop fundamental literacy skills in their chosen language, focusing on practical reading and writing capabilities required for daily life, form-filling, and basic communication.

**Target Learners:** First-generation learners, individuals with no formal education, rural learners, senior citizens seeking basic literacy.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Recognize and write alphabets and basic words
- Read and understand simple sentences and paragraphs
- Write basic information for forms and applications
- Comprehend everyday text (signs, labels, messages)

---

### 1.2 Everyday English

**Objective:** Develop practical English language skills for everyday communication, workplace interaction, and basic comprehension, enabling learners to navigate English-medium environments with confidence.

**Target Learners:** Students, job seekers, young adults, professionals seeking English proficiency.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Understand spoken English in common situations
- Read and comprehend English text at basic level
- Speak English with basic fluency for everyday conversations
- Write simple English messages and notes

---

### 1.3 Digital Skills

**Objective:** Build comprehensive digital literacy across devices, internet usage, applications, and digital safety, empowering learners to confidently use smartphones, computers, and online services for daily life and work.

**Target Learners:** First-generation digital users, senior citizens, rural learners, learners transitioning to digital platforms.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Operate smartphones and basic computer functions
- Navigate the internet and use web browsers
- Install and use mobile applications
- Practice digital safety and security
- Communicate using digital tools
- Perform online transactions

---

### 1.4 General Knowledge

**Objective:** Develop awareness across geography, history, current affairs, science, and cultural knowledge, with emphasis on Indian context, enabling learners to participate in informed discussions and competitive examinations.

**Target Learners:** Students, job seekers, competitive exam aspirants, general learners seeking knowledge enhancement.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Understand Indian geography and states
- Know key historical events and figures
- Comprehend basic Indian polity and governance
- Stay updated with current affairs
- Have awareness of science and technology developments

---

### 1.5 Career Skills

**Objective:** Equip learners with workplace readiness skills, job search capabilities, professional communication, and career development competencies required for employment and career growth.

**Target Learners:** Job seekers, young adults, career changers, skilled workers, students entering workforce.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Create effective resumes and job applications
- Search and apply for jobs effectively
- Perform well in interviews
- Communicate professionally in workplace
- Manage time and tasks efficiently
- Work collaboratively in teams

---

### 1.6 Financial Literacy

**Objective:** Develop understanding and management of personal finances, banking services, savings, budgeting, and financial decision-making, enabling learners to make informed financial choices and avoid fraud.

**Target Learners:** Homemakers, farmers, small business owners, job seekers, young adults, senior citizens managing pensions.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Use banking services effectively
- Create and manage personal budgets
- Save and plan for financial goals
- Use digital payments securely
- Understand loans, credit, and interest
- Access government financial schemes

---

### 1.7 Healthcare Literacy

**Objective:** Enable learners to understand health information, navigate healthcare services, read prescriptions and medical documents, and make informed health decisions for themselves and their families.

**Target Learners:** Senior citizens, homemakers, caregivers, rural learners, individuals managing chronic conditions.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Read and understand health information and prescriptions
- Navigate healthcare services (hospitals, clinics, pharmacies)
- Handle medical emergencies appropriately
- Understand basic nutrition and preventive care
- Use digital health services and telemedicine
- Manage medications effectively

---

### 1.8 Government Services

**Objective:** Empower learners to understand government services, rights, responsibilities, and access public services digitally, enabling them to avail benefits, schemes, and exercise their citizenship rights effectively.

**Target Learners:** Rural learners, senior citizens, first-generation learners, marginalized communities, individuals seeking government benefits.

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Understand and manage identity documents
- Access and apply for government schemes
- Use digital governance portals and apps
- Know rights and responsibilities as citizens
- Access public services (utilities, education, health)
- Navigate grievance redressal mechanisms

## 6. Difficulty Mapping

### 6.1 Difficulty Level Definitions

**Level 1: Foundation (Beginner)**
- **Learner Ability:** No prior knowledge or experience. Learner is completely new to the skill and needs step-by-step guidance.
- **Characteristics:** 
  - Requires concrete, familiar examples
  - Single-step operations only
  - Heavy visual support needed
  - Direct, explicit instructions
  - Minimal reading required
  - Immediate feedback essential
- **Question Style:** "What is this?" "Which button do you press?" "Identify the correct option"
- **Examples:** 
  - "Which button turns on the phone?" (with image)
  - "Select the letter 'A'"
  - "What is this called?" (pointing to object)

**Level 2: Basic (Elementary)**
- **Learner Ability:** Minimal prior knowledge. Learner has seen or heard about the skill but has not practiced it.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Familiar contexts with slight variations
  - Two-step operations
  - Mixed visual and text support
  - Basic application of knowledge
  - Some inference required
  - Practical scenarios
- **Question Style:** "How do you...?" "What happens when you...?" "Choose the correct step"
- **Examples:**
  - "You want to call your friend. Which app should you open?"
  - "Complete the word: C_T"
  - "What is the first step to send a message?"

**Level 3: Intermediate (Competent)**
- **Learner Ability:** Some prior experience. Learner has practiced the skill a few times and understands basic concepts.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Unfamiliar but relatable contexts
  - Multi-step operations
  - Text-dominant with optional visuals
  - Application and analysis required
  - Problem-solving involved
  - Hypothetical scenarios
- **Question Style:** "Why would you...?" "What is the best way to...?" "Compare these options"
- **Examples:**
  - "Your phone is running slowly. Which three actions could help?"
  - "Which sentence is grammatically correct?"
  - "You receive a suspicious email. What should you do?"

**Level 4: Advanced (Proficient)**
- **Learner Ability:** Substantial prior experience. Learner uses the skill regularly and understands nuances.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Unfamiliar and specialized contexts
  - Complex, multi-step problems
  - Text-dominant with minimal visuals
  - Analysis and evaluation required
  - Critical thinking essential
  - Abstract scenarios
- **Question Style:** "Evaluate the effectiveness of..." "Design a solution for..." "Analyze the trade-offs"
- **Examples:**
  - "Compare the security implications of pattern lock vs PIN"
  - "Rewrite this paragraph to be more professional"
  - "Create a budget for a family of four with ₹30,000 monthly income"

**Level 5: Expert (Mastery)**
- **Learner Ability:** Deep expertise. Learner has mastered the skill and can teach others or handle edge cases.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Highly specialized contexts
  - Complex, open-ended problems
  - Text-dominant, no visual support
  - Synthesis and creation required
  - High-level reasoning
  - Professional or academic scenarios
- **Question Style:** "Design a comprehensive strategy for..." "Critique this approach and propose improvements" "Synthesize multiple concepts to solve..."
- **Examples:**
  - "Design a digital security policy for a small business"
  - "Create a comprehensive career development plan for a mid-level professional"
  - "Analyze the impact of a new government policy on rural economy"

### 6.2 Skill-Specific Difficulty Progression

**Example: Smartphone Basics**

**Level 1 Questions:**
- Identify power button (image-based)
- Identify volume buttons (image-based)
- Recognize camera icon (image-based)
- "What do you press to turn on the phone?"

**Level 2 Questions:**
- "You want to take a photo. Which app do you open?"
- "How do you go back to the home screen?"
- "What happens when you press the volume button?"

**Level 3 Questions:**
- "Your phone screen is frozen. What are three ways to fix it?"
- "You want to save battery. Which settings should you change?"
- "How do you organize your apps into folders?"

**Level 4 Questions:**
- "Compare the battery life impact of different screen brightness levels"
- "Design a smartphone setup for an elderly family member"
- "Troubleshoot: Phone turns on but screen stays black"

**Level 5 Questions:**
- "Create a comprehensive smartphone security protocol for a family"
